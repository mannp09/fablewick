The brief set 10 to 30 percent as the target. The book lands at 7 percent overall, and no story clears 13 percent. That is a real ceiling, not an unfinished pass: these are already-published, professionally edited picture books with almost no throwaway prose, most padding was single filler words (very, just, whole, own, single), and a large share of each page is either a read-aloud repetition device (Kick breathe kick, come come come, Bear did not ask), a callback a later page depends on word for word, or dialogue that already carries the book's idea in a character's own voice, none of which this brief allows touching. Hands in the Soil, the most narrated and least dialogue-heavy of the 11, cut the most (13 percent) for that reason; The Girl Who Stopped Looking and Tuli's Whistle, built almost entirely from protected repeats and a load-bearing line, cut the least (5 percent). Every page-level number and reason for a lighter cut is stated inline in its own table and note, so this is a summary, not a hidden shortfall.

## What was deliberately not cut, and why

- The five triple-repeat devices used for read-aloud rhythm are kept at full or near-full length rather than cut to two: "Kick, breathe, kick" and "Splash, splash, splash" (Girl Who Stopped Looking), "up, and up, and up" (Pip and the Lost Cloud), "come, come, come" and "thought, and thought, and thought" (Hundred-Year Stone), the three "Bear did not..." lines (Quiet Bear), and Tuli's two PFFFFFF attempts. These are the read-aloud beats a 4-9 year old listens for; cutting them to save two or three words would cut the sound of the story, not its filler.
- Callback phrases that a page 5 depends on are protected rather than trimmed even where a generic edit would shorten them: "a little stern" (Hands in the Soil), "chair" (Boy Who Could Not Walk), "very, very scared / very, very scary" (Hundred-Year Stone), "Kick, breathe, kick" (Girl Who Stopped Looking), "getting ready" (Brave Little Seed). Cutting these would break the exact-word echo the continuity gain depends on.
- Central dialogue that carries the story's turn is left close to verbatim: Coach Ngu's "I'm asking you to swim your race" and "You swam your race" (Girl Who Stopped Looking), the squirrel's "Most things start as cannot-yet... that's where most foxes give up" (Tuli's Whistle), Franklin's "That is one thing. There are many other things" (Boy Who Could Not Walk). These lines are the closest the stories come to stating their own lesson out loud in-scene; reducing them further would either blunt the line or tip it into an explicit moral, which the house voice rule forbids either way.
- Two covers and two end lines (The Boy Who Couldn't Walk, Tuli's Whistle) are left unchanged. Both already run on a tight parallel structure ("Courage isn't standing up. Courage is showing up anyway"; "keeping going when the middle gets hard", where "middle" is load-bearing for a page 3 callback) where the only available cuts would either break the parallelism or remove the word the callback needs.
