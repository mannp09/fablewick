## What was deliberately not cut, and why

- Every name and place: Kabir, Ramanand, Gopaldas, Jivanji, Bharuch, Narmada, Gujarat, Karamsad, Vaal-Papadi. None were shortened, abbreviated, or swapped for a pronoun beyond what English already allows on a second reference.
- Every concrete image the pictures do not already carry alone: the twig turning from bare to green, the bare foot landing on the hidden boy, the lifted cloth revealing flowers instead of a body, the loom going click-clack under the weaver's hands, the green field standing alone in a dead-brown drought, the bowl of beans placed in the oldest woman's hands first.
- The repeated sound and rhythm devices that are part of the craft, not filler: "Step. Step. Step.", "Ram! Ram!", "Click, clack", "Oh... Oh." These were kept at or near their original length; cutting them would cut the sound the page is built around.
- The closing rhetorical questions that carry the lesson without stating it: who had truly taught and who had truly learned, where had the old weaver gone, why had no one ever thought to do this before. These are the moral working itself out in the child's or the listener's head; the plan forbids ever naming the moral outright, and these questions are the mechanism that keeps it unnamed.
- The exact number twenty-three years, repeated across three pages of Jivanji's story. A real figure like this is never softened to "many years" or rounded, in either direction, at any level.
- Dialogue content. Lines were tightened word by word (a repeated "he said" trimmed, a doubled clause folded to one) but no character's reasoning, request, or refusal was shortened into a different meaning.
- Pages already near the floor for their level: single-digit and low-teens word counts such as "By the river, the water went splash, splash," "Step. Step. Step," and "\"Ram!\" the teacher cried. \"Ram!\"" were left at or within one word of the original. Cutting further would have meant losing a beat or a name, which the plan forbids.
- The distinction between levels. Kids stays built on short plain sentences; seniors keeps its longer clauses and quieter pacing. Reduction tightened each register in its own voice rather than pulling every level toward the same shape.
